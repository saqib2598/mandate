# GOV.UK Service Standard

## Point 2 Solve a whole problem for users
<!-- tags: D7 D5 | nodes: MLFO D | factor: standards | domain: gov_services -->

Work towards creating a service that solves a whole problem for users, working with other teams and organisations where necessary. Design and develop your service around the needs of users, not around technologies or pre-selected solutions. This includes generative artificial intelligence and commercial off-the-shelf products. Users should not have to understand how government works in order to use public services.

## Point 3 Provide a joined up experience across all channels
<!-- tags: D6 D5 | nodes: D SINK | factor: deployment | domain: gov_services -->

Provide a joined up experience across all channels. Test all the parts of the service that the user interacts with, online parts and offline parts like letters. A person who cannot finish in the automated channel still needs a consistent route through the staffed channel.

## Point 4 Make the service simple to use
<!-- tags: D6 | nodes: D | factor: standards | domain: gov_services -->

Build a service that is simple, intuitive and comprehensible. And test it with users to make sure it works for them. Make sure the service helps the user to do the thing they need to do as simply as possible, so that people succeed first time, with the minimum of help.

## Point 5 Make sure everyone can use the service
<!-- tags: D11 D6 D4 | nodes: PP SINK | factor: sandbox | domain: gov_services -->

Make sure the service works with the most common assistive technologies, screen readers or speech recognition software for example, and test the service with disabled users and with older users. Meet level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines as a minimum. Think about accessibility from the start, including people who use a dialect or a language other than the one the model was tuned on.

## Point 14 Operate a reliable service
<!-- tags: D13 D5 | nodes: C SINK | factor: deployment | domain: gov_services -->

Operate a reliable service. When the digital channel fails, users still need to complete the task. Reliability includes a staffed fallback and a published account of performance, not only an uptime figure for the model host.
